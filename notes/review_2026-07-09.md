## What changed and why

### 1. `trips.py` + `skilled.py` — fill-stream continuity guard (highest impact)
**Before:** `trip_records` (and `skilled.round_trips`) trusted the fill stream
to be gapless: nothing checked that a fill's `startPosition` equals the previous
fill's resulting position. Two real gap sources exist: (a) `deep_fills` stops
paging at `MAX_DEEP_FILLS` (12,000) and then splices on the *recent*-2000 window,
leaving an unfetched hole in the middle for any account with a longer history;
(b) position changes with no fill (observed in 42% of *non-capped* cached
accounts). A trip straddling a gap silently absorbed everything after it —
wrong hold_h (up to the whole gap), entry/exit px, n_adds, pnl. Empirically:
87/243 cached deep-fills accounts show ≥1 BTC chain break; worst case
`0x820f…be71` had 757 breaks incl. a ~97-day gap. The module docstring claimed
"trips straddling a history edge are dropped" — that logic did not exist.
**Now:** both extractors track the previous fill's end position and, on a
mismatch (relative tolerance 1e-9), drop the open trip and wait for the next
flat-book fresh open, which is what the docstring always promised. Smoke test
on the worst-case account: longest hold 97 days → 7.8 days.

### 2. `trips.py` — per-trader `big%` used the wrong denominator
**Before:** `trader_summary` divided big-move entries by *all* trips, while the
pooled report divides by candle-readable trips only. Any trader with old
(pre-candle-window) trips had their big% silently diluted — e.g. 15 big of 20
readable out of 40 total printed 38% instead of 75%.
**Now:** denominator is the readable subset, matching the pooled stat; `None`
(printed `-`) when no trip has a candle read.

### 3. `whales_skill.py` — persistence test dropped opening fees
**Before:** `persistence()` filtered fills to `closedPnl != 0` *before* calling
`fill_metrics`, whose contract (its own docstring) is to receive the full list
so opening fees hit the equity curve. Every opening fill's fee was silently
missing from `net1`/`net2` → the split-half net-PnL persistence numbers
(Spearman +0.54..0.62, "85% of top half stay positive") were optimistically
biased. Fees only ever reduce PnL, so the bias is one-directional.
**Now:** the full fills stream is split at the median realized fill's timestamp
and both halves go to `fill_metrics` intact (with a guard for timestamp-tie
edge cases). Expect slightly less rosy persistence numbers on re-run.

### 4. `whales_track.py` — `--analyze` now skips degraded snapshots
**Before:** a rate-limited scan yields a near-empty whale list and writes
`skill_bias = 0.0` with valid prices — indistinguishable from a genuinely
neutral reading. `n_failed` was recorded (2026-07-02 review) precisely to
identify such rows, but `analyze()` never read it; `_pearson` would ingest the
artifacts (`_hit_rate` happens to skip zeros, so the two metrics would silently
disagree).
**Now:** new knob `MAX_N_FAILED = 75`; `--analyze` drops rows above it and
reports the count. Current series (284 rows): 0 dropped, results unchanged.
The ~2026-07-27 30-day re-check is now robust to degraded hours.

### 5. `trips_stats.py` — Stouffer step crashed on an empty trader set
**Before:** `z_st = sum(zs) / math.sqrt(len(zs))` → ZeroDivisionError when no
trader passes the ≥3 wins / ≥3 losses filter (latent today: 46/50 pass, but a
stricter `trip_filter` or smaller regenerated dataset could trigger it).
**Now:** early return with a message, matching the guards used elsewhere.

### 6. `btc_flows.py` — coinbase tx crash in `_counterparties`
**Before:** `i.get("prevout", {})` — but Esplora returns an explicit
`"prevout": null` for coinbase inputs (key present), so the entry became `None`
and `.get(...)` raised AttributeError on any coinbase tx paying a watched
address. The sibling `_addr_delta` and `btc_track.py` already guard this shape;
only this call site was missing it.
**Now:** `i.get("prevout") or {}`.

### 7. `btc/client.py` — `address_txs_paged` crashed on zero-history addresses
**Before:** with no confirmed txs, `out == []` but the while condition
(`0 < max_txs`) still ran `out[-1]` → IndexError. Dormant (no current callers)
but part of the client's public surface; `btc_flows._txs_since` guards the same
pattern correctly.
**Now:** `while out and len(out) < max_txs:`.

### 8. `skilled.py` — `--addr` with no argument crashed with IndexError
**Now:** prints a usage message via `sys.exit`.

## Rejected candidates (recorded so they aren't re-litigated)

- **leaderboard `.tmp` write race** (hl/client.py): two concurrent refreshes
  share one tmp path. Window is sub-second within a multi-second download,
  damage is transient (the second writer completes the file), and cron design
  already tolerates a lost point. Not fixed.
- **flip-fill fee/maker attribution** (trips.py + skilled.py): a flip fill's
  whole fee and maker flag go to the trip it closes; the opened trip starts
  clean. This is a *convention* (error bounded by one fill's fee), applied
  consistently in both extractors. Documented here instead of changed.
- **`n_failed` unused by `--show`**: display-only; not worth a column.

## Sonnet-subagent experiment notes (for the workflow decision)

- Sonnet reviewers: 4 parallel, ~308k subagent tokens total, 4–10 min wall
  clock. All 21 files (3,375 lines) read *outside* Fable's context; Fable read
  only the flagged regions (~300 lines) to verify.
- Precision: 12 reported → 7 real (58%). No false negatives found on
  spot-check, but the trips agent's empirical validation (running scripts
  against data/fills_deep/) exceeded what a text-only review would catch — the
  #1 finding came from that.
- Conclusion: raw token *total* is likely higher than a solo-Fable pass, but
  they're cheap Sonnet tokens, the wall clock is ~4x shorter, and Fable's
  context stayed small enough to do careful verification at the end. Good fit
  for this review-pass pattern specifically, as predicted.
